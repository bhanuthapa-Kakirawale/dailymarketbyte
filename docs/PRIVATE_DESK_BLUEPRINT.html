Private Intelligence Blueprint

How to learn, run, verify and use the Daily Market Byte private stack: the evening pipeline, the Private Trading Intelligence Desk, the Market Radar and the Market Regime classifier. Use it to decide what deserves your attention each day, with every claim traceable to stored data.

PRIVATE / LOCAL ONLY Desk Phase 1 · read-only Market Regime V1 · FROZEN / PROVISIONAL No orders · no uploads Today
Decision support, not advice. Everything the desk shows is an observation or a deterministic detector result. A Radar candidate is an attention item, and the market regime describes the current environment, not tomorrow. Nothing here has been validated as profitable yet (outcome analytics is a later phase). You make every decision, including sizing and risk.

Market Radar

Which stocks did something unusual?

Three independent evidence families: VOLUME (RVOL ≥ 3x; ≥ 5x is EXTREME), STRUCTURE (range breaks, SMA20/50 crosses), RELATIVE (5D and 20D vs NIFTY 50 beyond ±1.5 pp). Two families make a candidate NOTABLE, three make it HIGH INTEREST.
Market Regime

What kind of market is it?

BULLISH / BEARISH / NEUTRAL / TRANSITIONAL / INSUFFICIENT_DATA, from trend, breadth, sectors and volume, with volatility as a stress check. Deterministic rules with a WHY sentence. Context only: it never re-ranks the Radar.
Market Structure

How broad is the move?

NIFTY 200 advances / declines, unusual volume (≥ 2x), range events and sector breadth, always with the real denominator (for example 51 / 200).

03App training plan

Eight short modules, one page each. Do one or two per evening for the first week. A module is done when you can answer its self-check without looking.

Module 1 · ~10 min

The header and freshness strip

  • Read Latest completed market session vs Showing session.
  • Find each source's FRESH / STALE / MISSING badge.
  • Switch the session selector to an older day and notice the blue "earlier session" banner.

Self-check: what does STALE mean, and what is the fix? (The evening job hasn't covered the latest session: run run_evening_full.bat.)

Module 2 · ~15 min

The Market Regime card and the Regime page

  • Read the label, then the 7 dimension states, then Why.
  • Open detail →. For each dimension, find the numbers and the rule that produced its state.
  • In Recent regime history, find a * (unconfirmed change) and an APPROX row.

Self-check: what is the difference between NEUTRAL and TRANSITIONAL? Which dimensions can never decide the regime? (RELATIVE and FLOWS are context only; volatility can only block BULLISH.)

Module 3 · ~15 min

Dashboard: attention set and What changed

  • Read "N attention candidates from M Radar candidates". The attention set is a reading order, not a filter.
  • Learn the 5 tiers (see section 7) and read the "In attention set because" column.
  • Click each What-changed tile: it opens the Radar page with that filter applied.

Self-check: what decides the order inside a tier? (The existing Radar order: HIGH INTEREST first, then symbol. There is no hidden score.)

Module 4 · ~20 min

Stock intelligence page (A–H)

  • A Summary and WHY THIS STOCK: ✓ EVIDENCE VERIFIED vs VALUES UNAVAILABLE.
  • B Chart: toggle SMA20/50 and the prior 20/50-session high/low.
  • C Structure, D Volume (RVOL chart), E Relative vs NIFTY, F Sector + market regime, G Radar history, H Official events.

Self-check: why can Market Structure count a stock as "unusual volume" while the Radar's VOLUME family did not fire? (2x vs 3x thresholds.)

Module 5 · ~10 min

Radar page: the complete list

  • Sort by Day %, RVOL and families. Use the filter chips.
  • Download radar.csv: it includes reasons, metrics, official events and the session's market regime.

Self-check: which page is authoritative when the dashboard and the Radar disagree? (Radar: the dashboard is a subset.)

Module 6 · ~10 min

Sectors

  • NIFTY 200 sector breadth (NSE Industry column) vs NSE sectoral indices. These are separate universes and never mixed.
  • Check the reconciliation row: sector counts add up to the headline.

Self-check: is a Radar candidate's sector participating, or is the stock alone?

Module 7 · ~10 min

History

  • Filter by symbol, family, novelty and appearance over a date range.
  • Look for repeat names: FIRST RECORDED vs CONSECUTIVE vs REAPPEARED +Ns.

Self-check: how many times did your watched symbol appear in the last month?

Module 8 · ~10 min

Data Quality

  • Databases OK, Radar run COMPLETE, replay reconciliation MATCHED, Market Structure 100% PUBLISHABLE.
  • Regime classifier panel: status OK, universe POINT_IN_TIME, validation CURRENT, "point-in-time validation from".

Self-check: what would make you distrust tonight's page? (Anything not FRESH / OK / MATCHED; see section 9.)

07Stock-selection playbook: getting the most out of it

A disciplined way to turn the desk into a short research list. It narrows attention; it does not tell you what to do.

The core idea The desk gives you three questions answered with evidence: What kind of market is it? (regime), How broad is it? (Market Structure and sectors), and Which stocks did something unusual, and how? (Radar). The most useful names are usually where these three line up, or where a stock clearly diverges from them.
  1. Start with the regime, never with a stock Read the label and its Why before opening any candidate. It sets the bar for everything that follows.
    RegimeWhat it means for your review
    BULLISHTrend and breadth agree upward. Candidates aligned with the market (positive relative strength, upward structure events) have the wind behind them. Still verify each one.
    BEARISHMost stocks are weak. A stock with persistent positive relative performance stands out against the tide, so note it. Upside structure events here deserve extra confirmation.
    NEUTRALRange-like market. Range breaks matter more than drift; expect less follow-through from the index itself.
    TRANSITIONALEvidence conflicts or is changing. Raise your bar: ask for more evidence families and check whether the stock's sector participates. A * means a new state is not yet confirmed.
    INSUFFICIENTDon't lean on the regime. Judge candidates on their own evidence only.
  2. Read the attention set tier by tier 1STORY SELECTED (the Radar's own selector) 23 EVIDENCE FAMILIES 3VOLUME + STRUCTURE 4NEW (first recorded or back after N sessions) 5STATE CHANGE (quiet-day fill). Tiers 2–3 carry the most independent evidence; tier 4 is where new names first appear. Then scan the full Radar page for names in sectors you follow.
  3. Verify the evidence on the stock page before believing it
    • EVIDENCE VERIFIED (✓) means the numbers reproduce. VALUES UNAVAILABLE means treat it as unconfirmed.
    • Direction comes from the day % and the close, not from the family names.
    • Volume: UNUSUAL (≥ 3x) vs EXTREME (≥ 5x). Check the 60-session RVOL chart: is this the first spike or one of many?
    • Structure: a 50-session range break is a bigger event than an SMA20 cross. Check how far the close is beyond the level (break distance %).
    • Relative: PERSISTENT means both 5D and 20D vs NIFTY 50 point the same way, so it is not a one-day move.
  4. Check the context around the stock
    • Sector (section F): is the sector participating (breadth, median move, other candidates), or is the stock alone?
    • Market regime line (section F): is the stock moving with or against the regime? Write that down; both can be interesting for different reasons.
    • Radar history (section G): FIRST RECORDED, CONSECUTIVE or REAPPEARED. Repeated appearances with the same direction are persistence; a flip in direction is a change.
    • Official events (section H): F&O ban, ASM or GSM membership changes how a stock trades. Treat these as caution flags, and read the exchange's own wording.
  5. Apply your own filters (outside the desk)
    • Upcoming results or corporate actions, news, and liquidity for your size.
    • Your own entry plan, invalidation level and position size. The desk deliberately provides none of these.
  6. Write it down: 0 to 5 names, never forced On a day with nothing convincing, the right shortlist is empty. Record every name in the journal (section 8) with its evidence and the regime. That record is how you learn which setups work for you.

Alignment matrix (use it as a note, not a rule)

Candidate evidence upward (positive relative / upward structure)Candidate evidence downward
BULLISH regimeAligned: standard confirmation.Against the market: stock-specific weakness. Find out why.
BEARISH regimeAgainst the market: relative strength stands out. Demand persistence and volume.Aligned: standard confirmation.
NEUTRAL / TRANSITIONALNo tailwind either way. Weight the stock's own evidence, its sector's participation and how many families fired.

What the desk deliberately does not give you

No score, no ranking by "best", no buy/sell/hold, no target or stop, no forecast, and no hit-rate statistics yet. Regime-conditioned outcome research (for example "does Volume + Structure behave differently in BULLISH vs BEARISH sessions?") is Phase 2. It will use only point-in-time sessions; the first fully eligible session is expected to be 2026-10-15. Until then, your journal is the evidence.

Quality points - 10 components (PROVISIONAL, policy trade-candidate-score-2.0)

Every score carries score_validation_status = PROVISIONAL. Weights are a documented first-pass judgment call, never tuned against historical returns. A component that cannot be computed for a candidate returns status=NOT_SCORABLE, scored 0 - the denominator (100) is never renormalized, so a low total stays self-explanatory rather than hidden. "Direction" below is the Radar's own multi-session direction (see Direction); policy 2.0 kept 1.0's weights and bands and changed what A, C, I and the direction read (docs/PRIVATE_TRADE_CANDIDATES.md "Policy 2.0").

Direction and the long-only view

Research priority says nothing about direction - a downside breakdown can be ELIGIBLE. Every candidate shows the Radar's own multi-session direction (radar_direction, structure + relative evidence; volume never sets direction). Every directional reading in the score follows it, and the board defaults to upside setups only.

BadgeRadar evidenceLong-only reading
UPSIDEALIGNED_POSITIVE: structure and relative performance both point up. Ranked and shortlisted in the default view.
DOWNSIDEALIGNED_NEGATIVE: both point down.Information only (collapsed section). Not something a cash, long-only trader can act on.
MIXEDStructure and relative performance disagree.Information only, and never ELIGIBLE (capped at REVIEW: the score can only read the day's move as its direction). Conflicting evidence is not a long setup.
DAY AGAINSTThe session's own move went against the Radar direction (DAY_MOVE_AGAINST_RADAR_DIRECTION, rare).A pullback or bounce day inside the setup. The score still follows the Radar direction; look at where price closed against the structural reference.

Why it matters: in the stored 2026-10-16 snapshot all 8 ELIGIBLE candidates were downside breakdowns or mixed, and every upside breakout was REVIEW. ?view=ALL ranks every direction; ranking upside-only also stops a downside name from taking an upside name's sector slot in the shortlist.

Daily workflow - swing

  1. Start with the diversified shortlist, then the raw rankingBoth default to upside setups only. Diversification should not hide raw quality - a demoted name may still be the best setup, just concentrated in an already-full sector.
  2. Check eligibilitySkip anything DATA_INCOMPLETE or NOT_ACTIONABLE, and anything with a DAY AGAINST badge unless you have read why. Note capture_mode, candidate_as_of and quality_policy_version on the detail page - prefer a genuine POINT_IN_TIME capture from the stored snapshot.
  3. Read the setup, not just the totalOpen every component: setup quality, relative strength, sector alignment, MTF, regime alignment. A total can hide which components actually carried it.
  4. Check entry qualityextension_state (NORMAL/STRETCHED/DEEPLY_STRETCHED), pullback_state, range_position_pct. An ELIGIBLE candidate that is DEEPLY_STRETCHED or EXTENDED_FROM_STRUCTURE stays on the watchlist, not the trigger list - see the callout above.
  5. Check event proximityevent_proximity: NO_KNOWN_EVENT / EVENT_WITHIN_WINDOW (≤3 trading sessions either side) / EVENT_DATA_UNAVAILABLE, plus the RESULTS DATE? badge in results season. A known event changes nothing about direction - it is a reason to review, not a signal either way.
  6. Read the structural reference as research contextstructural_reference_price - for a breakout, the level it just cleared (BROKEN_PRIOR_20/50_HIGH); otherwise the prior 20-session low - and risk_width_label (NARROW/MODERATE/WIDE). A distance label, never an order - no stop-loss is placed anywhere in this system.
  7. Build a small next-session watchlistQuality over count - V1 does not prescribe a fixed number.
  8. Next morning: re-verify, don't assumeConfirm the candidate is still valid, no event/data flag materially changed, and the market/sector context still looks acceptable.
  9. During market hours: the score is not an entry triggerThe desk answers what deserves attention; exactly when to act belongs to a future execution layer that does not exist in this package.

  5. Review weekly/daily alignmentweekly_alignment ALIGNED vs DIVERGENT vs WEEKLY_INSUFFICIENT_DATA (fewer than 10 completed weeks).
  6. Compare raw ranking against the diversified shortlistA demoted name did not get worse - see raw vs. diversified below.
  7. Drop anything with poor data or live event riskDATA_INCOMPLETE, or EVENT_WITHIN_WINDOW on something you can't explain.
  8. Separate three listsReady to review now (NORMAL/AT_STRUCTURE) · extended / wait for a better location (STRETCHED or DEEPLY_STRETCHED) · watch-only (REVIEW, or ELIGIBLE with a caution flag).
  9. Prepare next week's focused watchlistFewer, explainable names over a long raw list.
  10. Check your own sector/position concentration manuallyThe diversified shortlist caps sector concentration at the candidate level (max 2 per sector); it knows nothing about your actual portfolio.

Raw ranking vs. diversified shortlist

Raw ranking

Every ELIGIBLE/REVIEW candidate, ELIGIBLE first, then by score

The full audit trail. Nothing removed, nothing re-scored. A REVIEW capped for partial data never outranks an ELIGIBLE candidate on score alone.
Diversified shortlist

Raw ranking, sector-capped

Walks the raw ranking and demotes (never deletes) a candidate once its sector already holds 2 shortlist entries, or - when enough trailing-return history exists (≥20 overlapping sessions in the last 60) - once it is ≥0.85 correlated with an already-shortlisted same-sector name. A candidate with no sector data is kept and listed as "diversification not applied".

A demoted name's score did not change. demotion_reason is always SECTOR_CONCENTRATION or HIGH_CORRELATION, with the exact detail (which sector, or which name it correlates with) shown alongside - never a silent re-rank.

Market Regime, sector context and multi-timeframe - read together, not separately

Market Regime

Context, not a command

BULLISH/BEARISH/NEUTRAL/ TRANSITIONAL/INSUFFICIENT_DATA (same classifier as section 7). regime_alignment: CANDIDATE_ALIGNS_WITH_REGIME / CANDIDATE_AGAINST_REGIME / REGIME_NEUTRAL. A strong setup against the regime loses alignment points - it does not erase the stock's own evidence.
Sector context

Is the sector moving its way?

Component C reads sector_vs_market_20d_pp: the sector's 20-session return minus NIFTY 50's, from a local equal-weight series of the stock's NIFTY 200 peers (the stock excluded). A strong stock in a strong sector is the cleaner long setup; a stock beating a weak sector earns no sector points. How far the stock beat its own sector (sector_relative_20d_pp) is shown as evidence only - never a claim about institutional behaviour.
Multi-timeframe

Does the week agree with the setup?

weekly_alignment: ALIGNED / DIVERGENT / WEEKLY_INSUFFICIENT_DATA (needs ≥10 completed ISO weeks; the current, still-forming week is always excluded). There is no MIXED or COUNTERTREND label - only these three.

Decision path

  1. Is it a Radar candidate?If not, it is outside this package's universe entirely.
  2. Is the Radar direction UPSIDE?DOWNSIDE or MIXED → information only for a long-only review (the default view already lists them separately).
  3. Data quality COMPLETE or PARTIAL, replay MATCHED?No → DATA_INCOMPLETE. Skip, or wait for the next refresh.
  4. Score ≥ 60 on COMPLETE data?No, but ≥35 or PARTIAL → REVIEW: read the detail page carefully. Below that → NOT_ACTIONABLE.
  5. Compounding-risk veto (DEEPLY_STRETCHED + EXTREME volatility)?Yes → NOT_ACTIONABLE regardless of score.
  6. Entry location acceptable (not DEEPLY_STRETCHED / EXTENDED_FROM_STRUCTURE)?No → keep on watch, don't chase.
  7. Event within 3 sessions, or structural risk WIDE?Yes → review with extra care; a known event is a reason to look closer, not a direction.
  8. Sector/MTF/regime acceptable?Mixed signals → weight the stock's own evidence more cautiously.
  9. Add to your own manual watchlist→ your own execution review → (future, not built) execution translation.

The tree never ends in "buy." It ends in a name on your watchlist for your own review.

When NOT to pick a candidate

Red / amber flags
  • Research priority is DATA_INCOMPLETE or NOT_ACTIONABLE (including a COMPOUNDING_RISK_VETO flag).
  • The Radar direction is DOWNSIDE or MIXED (an ELIGIBLE downside breakdown is still not a long setup), or the candidate carries the DAY AGAINST flag and you haven't read why.
  • capture_mode is RECONSTRUCTED, or the page shows a live re-evaluation, and you need a genuine point-in-time read.
  • extension_state = DEEPLY_STRETCHED or pullback_state = EXTENDED_FROM_STRUCTURE without a plan for that.
  • event_proximity = EVENT_WITHIN_WINDOW and you haven't reviewed what the event actually is, or the RESULTS DATE? badge is showing and you haven't checked the results date yourself.
  • risk_width_label = WIDE and that distance doesn't fit your own risk tolerance.
  • weekly_alignment = DIVERGENT while you're building a positional (not swing) idea.
  • regime_alignment = CANDIDATE_AGAINST_REGIME without extra confirmation.
  • Adding it to your shortlist would create excessive same-sector exposure, beyond what the diversified shortlist's own 2-per-sector cap already limits.
  • You can't explain the evidence in your own words from the detail page.

A high rank is never, by itself, a reason to act when any of the above is also true.

10Guardrails & red flags

Do not use tonight's view if…
  • any source is STALE or MISSING, or the evening verdict was ATTENTION REQUIRED;
  • replay reconciliation is not MATCHED for the names you care about;
  • the regime status is STALE or INSUFFICIENT_DATA (use it as no context at all);
  • a page says DATA UNAVAILABLE and "failed safely" (reload after the evening job finishes).
Interpretation traps
  • One-day breadth is not a regime: the regime uses 10-session and 50-session breadth.
  • An APPROX history row is a rough reconstruction (back-dated universe), not validation.
  • Market Structure "unusual volume" (≥ 2x) is wider than the Radar's VOLUME family (≥ 3x).
  • An F&O ban / ASM / GSM listing is an exchange surveillance status. Describe it in the exchange's terms, and treat it as a caution.
  • No intraday data: everything describes the completed session.
Hard boundaries (built in, test-enforced) The desk is read-only, local-only and has no broker connection. The Kite packet is context-only, with no order fields. Private intelligence never reaches the public YouTube Short. Keep it that way; don't share desk screenshots publicly.
